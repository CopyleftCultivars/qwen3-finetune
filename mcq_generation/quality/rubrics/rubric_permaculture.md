# Quality Rubric: Permaculture

**Domain scope:** Permaculture design principles, zones and sectors, guilds and companion planting, water harvesting, food forests, perennial polycultures, pattern recognition, ethical framework, design process.

## Dimension-Specific Anchors

### 1. Factual Correctness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Accurately describes design principles, ecological relationships in guilds, or permaculture systems. Correctly represents zone/sector analysis and pattern applications.

*Example:* "What is the primary purpose of **Zone 1** in permaculture design? **Answer: Intensive cultivation of frequently accessed plants**"

**Score 3:** Correct general principles but may oversimplify complex design considerations. Lacks context about site-specific adaptation.

**Score 1:** Misrepresents core principles, wrong ecological relationships in guilds, or promotes practices contradicting permaculture ethics (e.g., exploitative labor).

### 2. Question Clarity (1–5)

**Score 5:** Clear about design scale (garden, homestead, community). Specifies whether testing principles, patterns, or techniques. Contextualizes climate or site conditions when relevant.

*Example:* "In **temperate climates**, which tree is commonly used as a nitrogen-fixing support species in food forests?"

**Score 3:** Generally clear but could better specify context or design stage.

**Score 1:** Unclear whether asking about design principles vs. specific techniques. Missing critical site context.

### 3. Distractor Quality (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests understanding of permaculture principles vs. conventional approaches. Distractors reflect common beginner mistakes or conventional agriculture thinking.

*Example:* Q: "What principle guides water management in permaculture?"
- **A: Slow, spread, and sink water into the landscape** ✓
- B: Channel water away as quickly as possible (conventional drainage)
- C: Store all water in large tanks (ignores landscape infiltration)
- D: Rely on irrigation systems (tech-dependent approach)

**Score 1:** Obviously wrong with no educational value. Non-permaculture concepts.

### 4. Domain Relevance (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests core design principles, pattern recognition, guild relationships, zone/sector analysis, or permaculture ethics. Connects principles to practice.

**Score 3:** Tests specific techniques without connecting to underlying principles or patterns.

**Score 1:** Trivial details about specific species without design context. Name-dropping techniques without understanding.

### 5. Difficulty Appropriateness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Requires understanding of permaculture design principles and their application. Not answerable by conventional gardeners without permaculture training.

*Example:* "Why is **edge** important in permaculture design? **Answer: Maximizes ecological diversity and productivity**"

**Score 3:** Basic permaculture concepts from popular books (The One-Straw Revolution, Gaia's Garden).

**Score 1:** Obvious environmental benefits. Too specialized (regional native plants unknown outside locale).

## Category-Specific Red Flags

- **Missing ethics:** Ignoring care for earth, care for people, fair share
- **Tech-solutionism:** Promoting high-tech fixes over observation and pattern recognition
- **Decontextualized techniques:** Lists of companion plants without design rationale
- **Oversimplification:** "Plant X always goes with Y" without site specificity
- **Greenwashing:** Permaculture aesthetics without functional design

## Acceptance Thresholds

- **Composite ≥ 4.0:** Accept
- **3.5 ≤ Composite < 4.0:** Review for design principle connection
- **Composite < 3.5:** Reject (especially if technique-focused without principle understanding)
