# Quality Rubric: Indigenous and Traditional Knowledge

**Domain scope:** Traditional ecological knowledge (TEK), indigenous agricultural practices, seed saving and landrace cultivation, milpa/three sisters systems, traditional food sovereignty, cultural context of farming, biocultural diversity, intergenerational knowledge transfer.

## Dimension-Specific Anchors

### 1. Factual Correctness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Accurately describes traditional practices, respects cultural context, correctly represents ecological knowledge systems. Appropriately cites indigenous sources and contexts.

*Example:* "In the **three sisters** system (corn, beans, squash), beans provide **nitrogen fixation** to benefit corn growth"

**Score 3:** Generally correct about practices but may lack cultural context or oversimplify complex knowledge systems. Correct ecologically but incomplete culturally.

**Score 1:** Misrepresents traditional practices, romanticizes or essentializes indigenous knowledge, or promotes cultural appropriation without attribution. Factually wrong about ecological relationships.

### 2. Question Clarity (1–5)

**Score 5:** Specifies cultural or geographic context when relevant. Clear about whether testing ecological function vs. cultural practice. Respectful terminology and framing.

*Example:* "In **Mesoamerican milpa systems**, what is the traditional role of **squash** in the polyculture?"

**Score 3:** Generally clear but could provide more cultural context or specify regional variations.

**Score 1:** Unclear cultural context. Treats "indigenous" as monolithic. Missing geographic or cultural specificity when it matters.

### 3. Distractor Quality (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests understanding of ecological functions in traditional systems vs. conventional agriculture assumptions. Distractors reflect common misunderstandings of TEK.

*Example:* Q: "Why is seed saving central to traditional agricultural knowledge?"
- **A: Maintains genetic diversity adapted to local conditions** ✓
- B: Primarily for cost savings (economic reductionism)
- C: Prevents any genetic change (misunderstands adaptation)
- D: Only for sentimental reasons (dismissive of functional value)

**Score 1:** Obviously wrong about traditional practices. Disrespectful characterizations.

### 4. Domain Relevance (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests core concepts: polyculture ecological functions, seed sovereignty, traditional resource management, biocultural diversity, knowledge transmission, food system resilience.

**Score 3:** Tests specific practices without connecting to underlying ecological or cultural knowledge systems.

**Score 1:** Peripheral facts without knowledge system context. Exoticizing details. Trivia about specific ceremonies without agricultural relevance.

### 5. Difficulty Appropriateness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Requires understanding of traditional ecological knowledge and its applications. Not answerable by conventional agriculture knowledge alone. Respects complexity of indigenous knowledge systems.

*Example:* "How do landraces differ from modern hybrid varieties? **Answer: Selected over generations for local adaptation and trait diversity**"

**Score 3:** Basic concepts about traditional agriculture from popular permaculture books.

**Score 1:** Obvious cultural respect issues (e.g., "Is traditional knowledge valuable?"). Too specialized (specific ceremonial details without agricultural context).

## Category-Specific Red Flags

- **Cultural appropriation:** Extracting techniques without respecting knowledge systems or attribution
- **Romanticization:** "Noble savage" framing or idealizing without acknowledging complexity
- **Monolithic framing:** Treating all indigenous knowledge as the same
- **Ecological reductionism:** Stripping cultural meaning to extract only "useful" techniques
- **Deficit framing:** Comparing unfavorably to modern agriculture
- **Missing sovereignty dimension:** Ignoring land rights, seed sovereignty, or self-determination
- **Ahistorical treatment:** Ignoring ongoing indigenous innovation and knowledge development

## Acceptance Thresholds

- **Composite ≥ 4.0:** Accept (must include appropriate cultural context and respect)
- **3.5 ≤ Composite < 4.0:** Review for cultural appropriateness and attribution
- **Composite < 3.5:** Reject (especially if culturally inappropriate or lacks proper context)

**Critical considerations:**
- **Factual Correctness** must include cultural accuracy, not just ecological correctness
- **Domain Relevance** should privilege indigenous knowledge systems perspective
- Consider whether questions require consultation with cultural knowledge holders
