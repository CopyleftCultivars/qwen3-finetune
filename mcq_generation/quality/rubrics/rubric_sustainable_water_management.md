# Quality Rubric: Sustainable Water Management

**Domain scope:** Water conservation, irrigation efficiency, rainwater harvesting, watershed management, soil water retention, drought resilience, water quality protection, riparian management, irrigation scheduling.

## Dimension-Specific Anchors

### 1. Factual Correctness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Accurately describes water management principles, correct about irrigation methods and efficiency, sound watershed or water retention concepts, valid water quality considerations.

*Example:* "**Drip irrigation** typically achieves **85-95% application efficiency** compared to 60-75% for overhead sprinklers"

**Score 3:** Generally correct but may overstate water savings or oversimplify complex hydrological processes. Correct direction but imprecise magnitudes.

**Score 1:** Misrepresents water cycles, wrong efficiency claims, promotes practices that waste water or harm water quality. Incorrect about soil-water relationships.

### 2. Question Clarity (1–5)

**Score 5:** Specifies context (soil type, climate, crop). Clear about scale (field, farm, watershed). Distinguishes between water quantity vs. quality concerns.

*Example:* "In **semi-arid climates**, which practice most effectively increases **soil water retention**?"

**Score 3:** Generally clear but could specify climate, soil, or crop context better.

**Score 1:** Unclear scale or hydrological context. Missing critical details about water source or use purpose.

### 3. Distractor Quality (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests common misconceptions about irrigation efficiency, water conservation methods, or watershed processes. Includes plausible but incorrect water management strategies.

*Example:* Q: "What is a key principle of sustainable irrigation?"
- **A: Apply water based on crop ET and soil moisture** ✓
- B: Irrigate daily regardless of soil conditions (wastes water)
- C: Always irrigate to field capacity (can reduce root development)
- D: Use only overhead systems for uniformity (ignores efficiency)

**Score 1:** Obviously wasteful practices. Clearly unsustainable water use.

### 4. Domain Relevance (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests core water management concepts: conservation principles, irrigation scheduling, soil water dynamics, watershed protection, drought resilience strategies.

**Score 3:** Tests specific techniques without connecting to water conservation goals or hydrological understanding.

**Score 1:** Peripheral details (e.g., specific irrigation equipment brand names). Trivia without water management significance.

### 5. Difficulty Appropriateness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Requires specific knowledge of water management principles or irrigation science. Not answerable with common sense alone.

*Example:* "Why is **irrigation uniformity** important for water use efficiency? **Answer: Prevents over-watering some areas while under-watering others**"

**Score 3:** Basic water conservation concepts that environmentally-conscious people would know.

**Score 1:** Obvious (e.g., "Should we conserve water?"). Too specialized (specific emitter flow calculations).

## Category-Specific Red Flags

- **Tech-solutionism:** Promoting expensive irrigation tech without management focus
- **Ignoring soil:** Water management without soil health or infiltration consideration
- **Missing water quality:** Only addressing quantity without quality concerns
- **Scale inappropriate:** Field-level solutions for watershed-scale problems
- **Climate-blind:** Recommendations without climate context
- **Overlooking ET:** Irrigation without reference to crop water requirements

## Acceptance Thresholds

- **Composite ≥ 4.0:** Accept
- **3.5 ≤ Composite < 4.0:** Review for hydrological accuracy
- **Composite < 3.5:** Reject (especially if Factual Correctness < 3 on water principles)

**Critical:** Questions must be scientifically sound about water cycles and management—incorrect water advice can have serious consequences.
