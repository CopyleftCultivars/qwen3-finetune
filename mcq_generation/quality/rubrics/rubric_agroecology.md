# Quality Rubric: Agroecology

**Domain scope:** Ecological principles in agriculture, biodiversity, agroecosystem design, ecological pest management, polyculture systems, landscape ecology, farmer knowledge integration, food sovereignty, agroecological transitions.

## Dimension-Specific Anchors

### 1. Factual Correctness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Accurately describes ecological principles, system-level interactions, or agroecological practices. Correctly represents research on biodiversity-productivity relationships or ecological services.

*Example:* "Increased crop diversity in agroecosystems typically leads to **enhanced ecosystem services** including pest regulation and pollination"

**Score 3:** Correct principles but may oversimplify complex system dynamics. Generally accurate but lacks nuance about context-dependency.

**Score 1:** Misrepresents ecological relationships, wrong causal claims, or promotes practices contradicting agroecological principles (e.g., high external input dependency).

### 2. Question Clarity (1–5)

**Score 5:** Clear about scale (field, farm, landscape). Specifies whether asking about principles vs. practices. Contextualizes socio-ecological dimensions when relevant.

*Example:* "At the **farm-scale**, what is a key principle of agroecological design?"

**Score 3:** Generally clear but could better specify scale or context.

**Score 1:** Unclear whether asking about ecological, social, or economic dimensions. Missing critical context about system boundaries.

### 3. Distractor Quality (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests understanding of system-level thinking vs. reductionist approaches. Distractors reflect common industrial agriculture assumptions that contradict agroecological principles.

*Example:* Q: "What is a primary goal of agroecological pest management?"
- **A: Maintaining ecological balance to regulate pests** ✓
- B: Complete pest elimination (reductionist thinking)
- C: Maximum pesticide efficiency (industrial paradigm)
- D: Monoculture optimization (contradicts principles)

**Score 1:** Obviously wrong options with no plausibility. Non-ecological reasoning.

### 4. Domain Relevance (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests core agroecological concepts: system interactions, biodiversity functions, ecological resilience, farmer knowledge, agroecosystem design principles.

**Score 3:** Tests legitimate practices but disconnected from underlying ecological principles.

**Score 1:** Peripheral facts without system-level understanding. Trivial details about specific techniques without ecological rationale.

### 5. Difficulty Appropriateness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Requires understanding of ecological principles in agricultural contexts. Cannot be answered with conventional agriculture knowledge alone.

*Example:* "How do push-pull systems exemplify agroecological principles? **Answer: Integrate companion planting for pest regulation**"

**Score 3:** Basic concepts that permaculture enthusiasts might know.

**Score 1:** Obvious environmental benefits (e.g., "Is biodiversity important?"). Too academic (specific research citations).

## Category-Specific Red Flags

- **Reductionism:** Treating agroecology as just organic farming techniques
- **Missing social dimensions:** Ignoring farmer knowledge, food sovereignty, or equity
- **Scale confusion:** Not distinguishing field vs. landscape scale processes
- **Green revolution framing:** Using industrial agriculture assumptions
- **Depoliticizing:** Treating agroecology as purely technical rather than socio-ecological

## Acceptance Thresholds

- **Composite ≥ 4.0:** Accept
- **3.5 ≤ Composite < 4.0:** Review for system-level framing
- **Composite < 3.5:** Reject (especially if reduces agroecology to organic techniques)
