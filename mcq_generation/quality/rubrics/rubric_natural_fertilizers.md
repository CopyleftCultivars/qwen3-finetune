# Quality Rubric: Natural Fertilizers

This rubric provides category-specific guidance for evaluating MCQ quality in the **natural fertilizers** domain.

**Domain scope:** Animal manures, compost, vermicomposting, green manures, cover crops, biofertilizers (PGPR, mycorrhizae, nitrogen-fixing bacteria), natural mineral amendments, organic nutrient management.

## Dimension-Specific Anchors

### 1. Factual Correctness (1–5)

**Score 5 — Excellent:**
- Reflects current scientific consensus on nutrient content, application rates, or biological processes
- Correctly describes C:N ratios, decomposition rates, or microbial mechanisms
- Accurate representation of research findings or established practices

*Example:* "What is the typical C:N ratio for finished compost? **Answer: 10:1 to 15:1**" (widely accepted standard)

**Score 3 — Acceptable:**
- Generally correct but may oversimplify complex processes
- Uses approximate ranges where precision exists
- Correct in principle but lacking nuance

*Example:* "Green manures improve soil fertility" (true but vague about mechanisms)

**Score 1 — Unacceptable:**
- Incorrect nutrient values or application recommendations
- Misrepresents biological processes (e.g., nitrogen fixation, mineralization)
- Promotes practices that could harm soil or crops

*Example:* "Fresh manure should always be applied directly to growing vegetables" (unsafe due to pathogens)

### 2. Question Clarity (1–5)

**Score 5 — Excellent:**
- Specifies crop type, soil type, or climate when relevant
- Clear about timing (e.g., "before planting," "during active growth")
- Precise technical terminology (e.g., "mineralization rate" not "breakdown")

*Example:* "According to the principles of composting, what is the optimal moisture content for aerobic decomposition? **Answer: 50–60%**"

**Score 3 — Acceptable:**
- Generally clear but could benefit from more context
- Minor ambiguity that experts would resolve correctly

*Example:* "What is a benefit of using compost?" (unclear which benefit among many)

**Score 1 — Unacceptable:**
- Unclear whether referring to raw vs. composted materials
- Missing critical context (soil type, climate, crop)
- Vague about what aspect is being tested

*Example:* "What is the best organic fertilizer?" (no context for "best")

### 3. Distractor Quality (1–5)

**Score 5 — Excellent:**
- Distractors reflect common misconceptions (e.g., confusing C:N ratios, wrong application timing)
- Include plausible but incorrect nutrient values
- Test distinction between similar concepts (e.g., compost vs. vermicompost characteristics)

*Example:* Q: "What is the primary role of carbon in composting?"
- **A: Provides energy for microbial activity** ✓
- B: Supplies nitrogen for microbial growth (common confusion)
- C: Increases pH of finished compost (plausible misunderstanding)
- D: Prevents pathogen survival (related but wrong)

**Score 3 — Acceptable:**
- Some plausible distractors but at least one is obviously wrong
- Distractors vary in plausibility

**Score 1 — Unacceptable:**
- Obviously wrong options (e.g., "Compost is made from plastic")
- All distractors equally implausible
- Options that are technically correct in some contexts

### 4. Domain Relevance (1–5)

**Score 5 — Excellent:**
- Tests core principles: nutrient cycling, C:N ratios, application timing, biological processes
- Addresses practical decision-making (when to apply, how much, which material)
- Connects to soil health, crop nutrition, or environmental outcomes

*Example:* "Why is it recommended to incorporate green manure crops before full flowering?"
- Tests understanding of nutrient availability timing (highly relevant)

**Score 3 — Acceptable:**
- Tests legitimate domain knowledge but less central
- Useful information but not critical for practice

*Example:* "What color is finished vermicompost?" (observable characteristic, less conceptually important)

**Score 1 — Unacceptable:**
- Trivial details (e.g., historical dates, person names)
- Peripheral information (e.g., brand names of tools)
- No connection to nutrient management or soil health

### 5. Difficulty Appropriateness (1–5)

**Score 5 — Excellent:**
- Requires specific knowledge of nutrient dynamics or biological processes
- Cannot be guessed using common sense
- Appropriate for practitioners or advanced learners

*Example:* "What is the typical rate of nitrogen release from mature compost in the first year after application? **Answer: 10–20% of total N**"

**Score 3 — Acceptable:**
- Requires some domain knowledge but could be partially guessed
- Basic concepts that informed non-experts might know

*Example:* "Which of the following is a benefit of composting? **Answer: Reduces waste volume**"

**Score 1 — Unacceptable:**
- Answerable with common sense or general knowledge
- Obvious from process of elimination
- Too obscure (only researchers would know)

*Example:* "Does compost contain nutrients? **Yes**" (too easy)

## Category-Specific Red Flags

Watch for these issues specific to natural fertilizers:

- **Overgeneralization:** Treating all manures/composts as equivalent
- **Missing safety context:** Not mentioning pathogen concerns with fresh manure
- **Unrealistic precision:** Claiming exact nutrient percentages without noting variability
- **Ignoring soil testing:** Recommending rates without mention of soil analysis
- **Conflating terms:** Confusing compost, vermicompost, manure, humus, etc.
- **Climate-blind recommendations:** Not acknowledging climate effects on decomposition

## Acceptance Thresholds

**Recommended cutoffs for natural fertilizers category:**

- **Composite ≥ 4.0:** Accept
- **3.5 ≤ Composite < 4.0:** Review for minor improvements
- **3.0 ≤ Composite < 3.5:** Flag for revision (especially if Factual Correctness < 3)
- **Composite < 3.0:** Reject

**Critical dimensions:** Prioritize Factual Correctness (reject if < 3) and Distractor Quality (essential for valid assessment).
