# Quality Rubric: Integrated Farming Systems

**Domain scope:** Crop-livestock integration, integrated pest management (IPM), nutrient cycling across enterprises, diversified farm systems, agroforestry, aquaponics/integrated fish farming, system optimization, whole-farm planning.

## Dimension-Specific Anchors

### 1. Factual Correctness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Accurately describes system-level interactions, correctly represents IPM strategies, sound nutrient cycling principles, valid integration benefits and trade-offs.

*Example:* "In **crop-livestock integration**, manure provides nutrients while livestock consume crop residues, creating a **closed nutrient cycle**"

**Score 3:** Generally correct about component practices but may oversimplify system-level interactions. Correct about individual enterprises but vague about integration benefits.

**Score 1:** Misrepresents IPM principles (e.g., promotes prophylactic pesticide use), wrong about nutrient cycling, or unrealistic claims about integration benefits without trade-offs.

### 2. Question Clarity (1–5)

**Score 5:** Clear about which enterprises are integrated. Specifies scale (field, farm, landscape). Distinguishes between component performance vs. system-level outcomes.

*Example:* "In **silvo-pastoral systems**, what is a primary benefit of integrating trees with **pasture grazing**?"

**Score 3:** Generally clear but could better specify system boundaries or integration mechanisms.

**Score 1:** Unclear which enterprises are being integrated. Missing scale or temporal context. Conflates different integration types.

### 3. Distractor Quality (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests system-level thinking vs. enterprise-focused optimization. Distractors reflect conventional separated-enterprise assumptions.

*Example:* Q: "What is the primary goal of integrated pest management?"
- **A: Maintain pest populations below economic threshold** ✓
- B: Complete elimination of all pest species (unrealistic)
- C: Maximum pesticide application efficiency (misses IPM philosophy)
- D: Switching to organic pesticides only (input substitution, not integration)

**Score 1:** Obviously wrong about IPM or integration principles. Single-enterprise optimization answers.

### 4. Domain Relevance (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests core integration concepts: system-level synergies, nutrient cycling, IPM decision-making, enterprise complementarity, whole-farm optimization.

**Score 3:** Tests individual enterprise management without integration dimension.

**Score 1:** Peripheral to integration (e.g., specific livestock breed trivia). Single-enterprise facts unrelated to system benefits.

### 5. Difficulty Appropriateness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Requires understanding of system-level interactions and integration principles. Not answerable by single-enterprise specialists without integration experience.

*Example:* "Why is **timing** critical in crop-livestock integration? **Answer: Coordinating grazing with crop cycles to maximize residue use and minimize soil compaction**"

**Score 3:** Basic integration concepts (e.g., "livestock provide manure").

**Score 1:** Obvious system benefits. Too specialized (specific enterprise optimization details).

## Category-Specific Red Flags

- **Reductionism:** Treating integrated systems as simple sum of parts
- **Missing trade-offs:** Only mentioning benefits without acknowledging management complexity
- **Scale confusion:** Not distinguishing rotation-based vs. spatial integration
- **IPM misrepresentation:** Treating IPM as "use less pesticide" rather than ecological management
- **Unrealistic synergies:** Claiming integration solves all problems
- **Ignoring labor/management:** Not acknowledging increased coordination requirements

## Acceptance Thresholds

- **Composite ≥ 4.0:** Accept
- **3.5 ≤ Composite < 4.0:** Review for system-level framing
- **Composite < 3.5:** Reject (especially if treats enterprises independently rather than integrated)
