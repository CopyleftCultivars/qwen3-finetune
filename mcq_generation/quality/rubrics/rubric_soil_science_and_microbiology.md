# Quality Rubric: Soil Science and Microbiology

**Domain scope:** Soil structure, soil biology, microbial ecology, mycorrhizal fungi, soil food web, nutrient cycling, soil health indicators, rhizosphere processes, decomposition dynamics.

## Dimension-Specific Anchors

### 1. Factual Correctness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Accurately describes soil processes, microbial mechanisms, or ecological relationships. Correctly uses technical terms (rhizosphere, hyphae, mineralization, aggregation). Reflects current microbiology understanding.

*Example:* "Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi form **intraradical hyphal structures** inside root cortex cells" (accurate morphology)

**Score 3:** Generally correct but may oversimplify complex microbial interactions. Correct in broad strokes but lacks precision.

**Score 1:** Misrepresents microbial processes, incorrect soil chemistry, wrong ecological relationships, or outdated understanding of soil biology.

### 2. Question Clarity (1–5)

**Score 5:** Specifies soil type, conditions (aerobic/anaerobic), or microbial groups when relevant. Clear about spatial scale (aggregate, rhizosphere, bulk soil).

*Example:* "In **well-aerated soils**, which microbial group dominates organic matter decomposition?"

**Score 3:** Generally clear but could specify conditions or scale more precisely.

**Score 1:** Unclear whether referring to bacteria, fungi, or archaea. Missing context about soil conditions or temporal dynamics.

### 3. Distractor Quality (1–5)

**Score 5:** Distractors test common confusions (bacteria vs. fungi roles, types of mycorrhizae, nutrient forms). Include plausible mechanisms that occur in different contexts.

*Example:* Q: "What is the primary benefit of mycorrhizal associations for plants?"
- **A: Enhanced phosphorus uptake** ✓
- B: Nitrogen fixation (confuses with rhizobia)
- C: Direct carbon transfer to plants (backwards)
- D: Protection from all pathogens (overstated)

**Score 1:** Obviously wrong options about basic microbiology. Non-existent processes or organisms.

### 4. Domain Relevance (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests core concepts: microbial functions, nutrient transformations, soil structure formation, symbiotic relationships, soil health principles.

**Score 3:** Tests legitimate but peripheral knowledge (specific organism names without functional understanding).

**Score 1:** Trivial taxonomy without ecological context. Memorization of terms without process understanding.

### 5. Difficulty Appropriateness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Requires understanding of microbial ecology, soil processes, or functional relationships. Not answerable with general biology knowledge.

*Example:* "What soil condition favors denitrification over nitrification? **Anaerobic conditions**"

**Score 3:** Basic soil biology that educated gardeners might know.

**Score 1:** Obvious to anyone with basic biology (e.g., "Do soils contain microorganisms?"). Too specialized (specific enzyme pathways).

## Category-Specific Red Flags

- **Anthropomorphizing microbes:** Attributing intent to microbial behavior
- **Oversimplifying networks:** Treating soil food web as simple linear chains
- **Ignoring conditions:** Making claims without specifying aerobic vs. anaerobic, pH, temperature
- **Confusing types:** Mixing up ectomycorrhizal vs. arbuscular mycorrhizal characteristics
- **Outdated models:** Using superseded understanding of microbial ecology

## Acceptance Thresholds

- **Composite ≥ 4.0:** Accept
- **3.5 ≤ Composite < 4.0:** Review (especially Factual Correctness and Clarity)
- **Composite < 3.5:** Reject or major revision

**Critical:** Factual Correctness must be ≥3 (microbial misinformation is harmful).
