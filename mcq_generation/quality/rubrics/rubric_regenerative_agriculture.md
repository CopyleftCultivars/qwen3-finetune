# Quality Rubric: Regenerative Agriculture

**Domain scope:** Soil health building, carbon sequestration, holistic management, adaptive multi-paddock grazing, cover cropping, no-till/reduced-till, perennial integration, ecosystem restoration, ranch/farm resilience.

## Dimension-Specific Anchors

### 1. Factual Correctness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Accurately describes regenerative practices, soil health indicators, carbon cycling, or holistic management principles. Correct about grazing impacts, cover crop benefits, or no-till effects.

*Example:* "Adaptive multi-paddock grazing increases **soil organic matter** through trampling of vegetation and stimulated root turnover"

**Score 3:** Generally correct but may overstate carbon sequestration rates or oversimplify complex outcomes. Correct direction but imprecise magnitude.

**Score 1:** Misrepresents soil health impacts, wrong about grazing effects, or makes unsupported claims about carbon sequestration rates.

### 2. Question Clarity (1–5)

**Score 5:** Clear about practice (grazing, cover cropping, etc.). Specifies context (soil type, climate, livestock type). Distinguishes short-term vs. long-term outcomes.

*Example:* "In **adaptive multi-paddock grazing**, what is the primary goal of **short grazing periods**?"

**Score 3:** Generally clear but could specify timeframe or context better.

**Score 1:** Unclear whether referring to continuous vs. rotational grazing. Missing critical management context.

### 3. Distractor Quality (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests common confusions (rotational vs. continuous grazing outcomes, cover crop vs. cash crop purposes). Includes plausible but incorrect management goals.

*Example:* Q: "What is the primary soil health benefit of cover cropping?"
- **A: Increases organic matter and prevents erosion** ✓
- B: Eliminates need for crop rotation (overstated)
- C: Replaces all fertilizer needs (unrealistic)
- D: Prevents all pest problems (too broad)

**Score 1:** Obviously wrong about regenerative principles. Conventional agriculture misconceptions.

### 4. Domain Relevance (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests core regenerative concepts: soil health principles, grazing management, cover cropping systems, carbon cycling, holistic decision-making.

**Score 3:** Tests specific techniques without connecting to underlying soil health or ecosystem function.

**Score 1:** Peripheral details. Buzzwords without understanding. Marketing claims vs. practice.

### 5. Difficulty Appropriateness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Requires understanding of regenerative principles and their application. Not answerable by conventional farmers without regenerative agriculture exposure.

*Example:* "Why is **livestock integration** important in regenerative systems? **Answer: Nutrient cycling and soil disturbance for renewal**"

**Score 3:** Basic concepts from popular regenerative agriculture books (Dirt to Soil, The Carbon Farming Solution).

**Score 1:** Obvious environmental benefits. Too specialized (specific soil aggregate measurements).

## Category-Specific Red Flags

- **Greenwashing:** Using "regenerative" label without soil health focus
- **Carbon tunnel vision:** Overemphasis on sequestration without broader ecosystem health
- **Technique fetishization:** Specific practices (e.g., no-till) as dogma without context-sensitivity
- **Overstated timelines:** Unrealistic claims about how quickly soil health improves
- **Ignoring economics:** Practices without consideration of farm viability
- **Missing monitoring:** Not addressing adaptive management through observation

## Acceptance Thresholds

- **Composite ≥ 4.0:** Accept
- **3.5 ≤ Composite < 4.0:** Review for soil health connection
- **Composite < 3.5:** Reject (especially if promotes practices without soil health outcomes)
