# Quality Rubric: Organic Farming

**Domain scope:** Organic certification standards, soil fertility management, pest and disease management, weed control, crop rotation, organic inputs, transition to organic, market considerations, record-keeping.

## Dimension-Specific Anchors

### 1. Factual Correctness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Accurately describes organic standards (USDA NOP, EU, IFOAM), allowed/prohibited inputs, or organic management practices. Correct about transition periods and certification requirements.

*Example:* "What is the **transition period** for annual crops under USDA organic standards? **Answer: 36 months before harvest**"

**Score 3:** Generally correct but may not specify which standard (USDA vs. EU vs. other). Correct practices but vague about certification details.

**Score 1:** Wrong about certification requirements, promotes prohibited inputs, or misrepresents organic principles vs. bureaucratic standards.

### 2. Question Clarity (1–5)

**Score 5:** Specifies certification program when relevant (USDA, EU). Clear about context (certified vs. principles-based organic). Distinguishes between requirements vs. best practices.

*Example:* "Under **USDA NOP standards**, which practice is **prohibited**?"

**Score 3:** Generally clear but could specify certification context or requirements vs. recommendations.

**Score 1:** Unclear whether asking about legal requirements vs. recommended practices. Missing certification context when it matters.

### 3. Distractor Quality (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests common confusions about allowed/prohibited inputs, transition requirements, or certification processes. Includes plausible-sounding but incorrect practices.

*Example:* Q: "Which input is **allowed** under USDA organic standards?"
- **A: Copper sulfate (limited use for disease control)** ✓
- B: Synthetic NPK fertilizers (prohibited)
- C: Neonicotinoid insecticides (prohibited)
- D: GMO seeds (prohibited)

**Score 1:** Obviously prohibited inputs. Practices clearly outside organic philosophy.

### 4. Domain Relevance (1–5)

**Score 5:** Tests core knowledge needed for organic management: certification requirements, allowed inputs, rotation principles, organic pest management, transition planning.

**Score 3:** Legitimate organic practices but not certification-critical (cosmetic issues, minor optimizations).

**Score 1:** Peripheral to organic management. Trivial record-keeping details. Market trivia.

### 5. Difficulty Appropriateness (1–5)

**Score 5:** Requires specific knowledge of organic standards or management practices. Not answerable by conventional farmers without organic training.

*Example:* "What is the maximum copper application rate under **NOP standards**? **Answer: Based on label restrictions**"

**Score 3:** Basic organic concepts from popular organic gardening books.

**Score 1:** Obvious (e.g., "Does organic farming avoid synthetic pesticides?"). Too specialized (obscure exemption clauses).

## Category-Specific Red Flags

- **Standard confusion:** Mixing USDA, EU, and IFOAM requirements
- **Principles vs. compliance:** Treating organic as paperwork rather than ecological farming
- **Outdated information:** Using pre-2002 NOP or older standards
- **Input-substitution framing:** "Organic = replace synthetic with natural" without system redesign
- **Missing philosophy:** Ignoring soil health focus, treating organic as input restrictions only

## Acceptance Thresholds

- **Composite ≥ 4.0:** Accept
- **3.5 ≤ Composite < 4.0:** Review for standard accuracy
- **Composite < 3.5:** Reject (especially if Factual Correctness < 3 on certification requirements)
